# Copy angles (all Black Card, word for word)

Source: `research/limitless-black-card/BLACK-CARD-COPY-2026-10-29.md`. Pick one angle per ad; open on its pain line; then product; then proof; then end card. Don't open on a line in `used-lines.md`.

| Angle | Opening pain (pick one) | Turn / product lines | Pairs with proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Bigger = less efficient** (HDR-001, PT1) | The bigger your marketing agency gets, the more inefficient it becomes. / Every pod fills up with reports, approval chasing and "where are we on this?" messages, and payroll climbs faster than revenue. | You don't know what you don't know, and owners only hand agents the jobs they remember. / The fix is to find the hours first, then hand them to agents. | method, agents live |
| **B. Automated the wrong things** (HDR-002, PT2) | Everything runs pretty much the same, except now your Claude or ChatGPT bill costs you thousands a month. / But you still haven't seen your overhead come down, or your team's capacity go up. | An automation agency asks you what's eating up your team's time, then builds whatever you think the biggest time-waster is. / But when you have a team of 20, 40, 60-plus people, you're not in the work deep enough to know what's really costing your team the most hours. / We install actual AI workers into your agency, and we don't put it on you to figure out what needs to be built. | small team ops, Amazon agency |
| **C. Best people, busywork** (HDR-003, PT3) | You're paying for strategy and getting $20-an-hour work. / Every day, they're pulling numbers from three ad accounts into client decks. / Chasing clients for creative approvals they've already asked for twice. / Answering the same question in Slack about a launch date that's already in ClickUp. | Talented people don't leave because the work is hard. They leave because the work is beneath them. / When they go, the client relationships and everything in their head walk out the door with them. / Reports build themselves. Client updates go out without anyone writing them. | one job, our own team |
| **D. Six-figure payroll** (landing + thank-you) | Get six-figure work out of your six-figure payroll again, while AI agents handle the busywork. / Every new client brings new busywork, and most of it lands on your best people. / Your best people end up doing busywork, and payroll climbs faster than revenue. | AI agents take the reports, approval chasing and status updates off your team's plate, so their hours go back to strategy, creative and keeping clients. / We see how work actually moves through Slack and your team's apps, and nobody fills in a timesheet. | our own team, one operator |
| **E. ChatGPT isn't it** (HDR-001 + HDR-003) | But most owners don't know where to start. So the team uses ChatGPT for emails and summaries, and nothing really changes. | These aren't chatbots your team types prompts into. They're actual workers inside your agency. / These agents aren't your team typing prompts. They're actual workers inside your business, doing the mundane work the same way every time. | Amazon agency, agents live |
| **F. Jobs owners forget** (PT1 headlines) | The hard part is knowing which jobs to hand over. / Owners only hand agents the jobs they remember. | So we find the hours first. That's the Time Recovery Method. / First, we measure. ... (one quick beat at most) | method, audit catch |
| **G. Booking call** (booking + thank-you) | Pick a time below, and we'll show you where agencies like yours lose the most hours to busywork, and how you'd get them back. | On this call, we'll show you how your team could take on more clients without another hire. | same-day onboarding |

Meta headlines (section 02) work as on-screen card headers or chapter labels, e.g. "Why your AI spend isn't saving hours", "Paying for strategy, getting busywork", "The jobs owners forget to automate".

Measure / automate / recover: at most one quick beat. Salman found the 3-step middle repetitive across ads.
